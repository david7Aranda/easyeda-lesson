Every part, by schematic sheet, with the LCSC number to search in EasyEDA

Four sheets, one project. A net label with the same name is the same wire on every sheet.

1Open the library
Press Shift+F, or click Library in the left panel.
2Paste the LCSC number
Type it in the search box, for example C14663, and press Enter.
3Check the match
Pick the LCSC / JLCPCB result whose MFR part matches this list.
4Place it
Click Place, then click on the sheet. Edit › Annotate renumbers C1, R1 and the rest.
Search the C-number, not the value. "100nF" finds thousands of parts. "C14663" finds one, with its footprint and 3D model linked, and it's the number JLCPCB assembles from.
Basic or Extended? Each different Extended part adds a loading fee if JLCPCB assembles the board. Check: confirm it in the search result. Hand-soldering? Ignore it.

Why 27 pF on the 8 MHz crystal

Each cap = 2 × (CL − Cstray) = 2 × (20 − about 6.5) ≈ 27 pF.

The Basic 22 pF (C1653) also works. The clock runs a few tens of ppm fast, which USB and UART don't notice.

Will Y1 start? Yes

gm,crit = 4 × ESR × (2πF)² × (C0 + CL)² ≈ 0.43–0.59 mA/V for C0 of 3–7 pF.

The F446 allows up to 1 mA/V, so it passes. Y2 is the Nucleo's own LSE crystal with the same 4.3 pF caps; use LSE high drive.

If a part is out of stock

Search the same value and package, for example "100nF 0603 X7R", and pick a Basic part.

Keep the voltage rating the same or higher, and keep C0G on the crystal caps.

The one firmware change between the boards is the HSE setting in CubeMX's RCC page. Forget it and our board's crystal never starts.

Program our board with the Nucleo's ST-LINK

  1. Take off both CN2 jumpers. That disconnects the ST-LINK from the Nucleo's own chip.
  2. Wire CN4 to J2, pin 1 to pin 1 through pin 6 to pin 6.
  3. Power our board from its own USB-C. CN4 pin 1 only senses our 3.3 V; it doesn't supply it.
  4. Connect in STM32CubeProgrammer or CubeIDE over SWD. If it won't connect, choose "Connect under reset" (that's why NRST is on pin 5).
  5. Put both CN2 jumpers back to program the Nucleo again.

First firmware, on the Nucleo

  1. New STM32 project, board NUCLEO-F446RE.
  2. Blink PA5 with HAL_GPIO_TogglePin and a 500 ms HAL_Delay.
  3. Read PC13: the LED changes when B1 is pressed (it reads 0).
  4. Send "hello" with HAL_UART_Transmit on USART2 at 115200 baud and read it in a serial terminal.
  5. Save it. This becomes the bring-up test for our board.
